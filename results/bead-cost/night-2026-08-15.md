# The night of 2026-08-15: three lanes, and an instrument that had to be rebuilt around them

**What the night bought: the admission gate discriminates, and it is not at ceiling.** That was the pilot's central worry and the reason the whole experiment was in doubt. With three lanes and enough runs, the gate returns admitted, wrong, no-diff and truncated on the same bead, and one lane fails it in a consistent, identifiable way.

**What it cost: most of the hours went into the harness, not into the models.** Seven defects were found, every one of them in the environment rather than in a lane, and four of them would have quietly corrupted every number produced afterwards.

## The lanes

|---|---|---|---|---|

| **gemini-3.7-flash-medium** via `agy` | 8 | **3** | 1153 s | one turn always |
| **glm-5.2** via `pi` | 6 | **1** | 753 s | four stopped without a diff |
| **deepseek-v4-pro-max** via `pi` | 1 | 0 | 474 s | one run, killed by the lane's output ceiling; parked |

### The wrong answer belongs to the bead, not to a lane

Every rejected run - on **both** harnesses and **three** different models - fails identically: it decodes in the callback that receives an **already-parsed** URL. `&amp;` survives that, because it is not a URL delimiter; `&#38;` and `&#x26;` do not, because a parser cuts at `#` before the callback ever sees the href. The verdict is always `a1 true, a2 false, a3 true, a4 false, a5 false`, and the observed requests always pair `/p/named?x=1&y=2` correct with `/p/hex?x=1&` truncated.

This is exactly the architecture the pilot recorded as the plausible-wrong answer, the one a hand-written probe fell into without trying. **It is an attractor of the problem rather than a weakness of any lane.** kimi landed in it once in five, gemini five times in eight. What separates the lanes is how often they are caught, not what catches them - and a bead whose wrong answer is this reproducible is a better instrument than one whose failures scatter.

### glm argues before it implements, and can do both

Four glm runs ended with `stopReason: stop` after 80-odd turns, having written no code, to argue that the bead's prescribed fix cannot meet its own acceptance criteria. The last run of the night then implemented it and was admitted. So the objection is not a capability ceiling; whether it is *correct* is a separate question worth answering rather than dismissing, since three other lanes did satisfy those criteria.

### kimi completes, and per run it is the expensive way to complete

Four for four on admission, at 122 to 474 turns against gemini's single turn. One of those runs is also the one that patched the machine's `spider` crate, so its turns include work spent inside a dependency, and its cost is not comparable with a run that stayed in the repository.

### A lane that dies mid-edit is not a model that answered wrongly

One gemini run spent 110 minutes, was killed by the harness with `timeout waiting for response`, and left 306 uncommitted lines in a tree that did not build. Nothing scored it: its verdict reads `scored: false`. Because the tree was dirty it was filed as a rejected fix, which put a harness failure in the denominator of that lane's cost. It is now `aborted` and sits with the poisoned window rather than with the model's own outcomes. The distinction is invisible in the tree - both look like an unfinished edit - and is readable only as a missing verdict beside a harness reporting its own error.

## Cost per completed bead

**A run the model failed is part of the price of the bead it did not deliver.** A rejected fix is not free: someone reviews it, finds it wrong, and pays for the work a second time, whether that someone is a person or the same model on a later attempt. So `wrong`, `no-diff` and `truncated` stay in the numerator, and only the instrument's own failures leave it - a poisoned window, a lane that was never reached, a lane that died mid-edit. The unit that follows is attempts per admitted bead, and unlike tokens it is a count of runs, which every harness reports the same way.

| lane | attempts | admitted | wrong | no-diff | admission rate (95% CI) | **attempts/bead** | **wall/bead** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **kimi-k2.7** | 5 | 4 | 1 | 0 | 80% [38-96] | **1.25** | 2184 s |
| **gemini-3.7-flash-medium** | 8 | 3 | 5 | 0 | 38% [14-69] | **2.67** | 2820 s |
| **glm-5.2** | 6 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 17% [3-56] | **6.00** | 5487 s |
| **deepseek-v4-pro-max** | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0% [0-79] | undefined | undefined |

Twenty usable attempts, eight admitted beads, about six and a half hours of model time.

### Per run and per bead point opposite ways

The section above records kimi as the expensive way to complete, and per run that is what the turn counts say. Per bead delivered it is the cheapest of the three, by a margin that is not subtle. On the `pi` lane the two models share a meter, so their tokens are comparable with each other:

| | input/bead | output/bead | turns/bead |
|---|---|---|---|
| kimi-k2.7 | 37.1 M | **105 k** | 273 |
| glm-5.2 | 52.4 M | **332 k** | 658 |

**glm spends 3.1 times kimi's output to deliver the same bead**, and not because its runs are long - its median run is the shortest of the night at 753 s. Five of its six produce nothing. Removing `kimi27-02`, the run that went into the crate, moves kimi to 108 k: the outlier is not what carries the conclusion.

### What the numbers cannot yet carry

- **The intervals all overlap.** 38% against 80% does not separate gemini from kimi at these counts. Five runs a lane buys an order of magnitude on cost, which is what it was for; it does not buy a ranking. Separating a 40-point difference at 80% power needs roughly 23 runs a lane.
- **glm's denominator is 1.** A second admitted run halves its cost per bead, from 6.00 to 3.00. It is the least stable figure in the table by a wide margin.
- **The four glm `no-diff` runs are a different kind of cost**, and possibly not a cost at all. Nothing was produced to review, and if the objection those runs raise about the bead is correct, they are the bead's failures rather than the model's. That open question moves this table, which is a reason to answer it.
- **Tokens still do not compare across harnesses.** `agy` reports one envelope total per run while `pi` sums per-turn input, which counts the whole prompt again every turn, and `agy` reports cache reads where the Ollama lanes do not report them at all. Dividing one by the other would produce a number, not a measurement. Every per-run figure is in `evals/bead_cost/tabulate.py --json`.

## The seven defects, and what each would have cost

| # | defect | what it would have done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Runs were worktrees sharing the subject's ref namespace | `git log --all` opened with earlier runs' fixes, naming both the fix and its layer |
| 2 | The rubric and the answer key were readable during a run | the jail was checked, the runs were launched outside it |
| 3 | The agy lane's credential was never copied | the lane failed auth in two seconds and produced no data point |
| 4 | The collector read token keys the harnesses do not write | a well-formed zero across 28 turns, which reads as a measurement |
| 5 | One scoring build directory across identical clones | one tree's binary ran another tree's test, stably and plausibly |
| 6 | A run patched `spider` in the shared cargo registry | the subject bead was fixed for every later build, including the scorer's |
| 7 | The build volume filled | runs failed instantly in a loop, each burning an id and a strike |

Defects 5 and 6 are the ones worth remembering. Both produce a **stable** wrong answer, and stability is what made them survive: four identical re-scores agreeing on a wrong verdict reads exactly like a solid measurement. What separated them from a real result was the negative control - score a tree known to be unfixed and confirm the check can fail - which is now the first step in the manual rather than a step in it.

## One rule the night produced

**Share what is immutable, isolate what gets written.** It arrived three times in three costumes: cargo's extracted sources, npm's bookkeeping, and the scorer's build directory. Every run isolates its own state and then deliberately shares the machine's caches, because an empty cache is a tax the first run pays and the rest do not. Sharing is right and it is not free, and the part that makes it expensive is that **the failure never implicates the cache** - it arrives as an accusation against the run: a model that cannot build, a lane that does not exist, a base tree that passes.

## Open

- **`deepseek`**, parked. The cause of its failure is fully measured: `pi` sends `max_completion_tokens`, which is silently ignored on the path to Ollama Cloud, so no output limit is applied and the model ran to the provider's hard 65 536 ceiling with `reasoning_effort: max` forced by the proxy. Raising the limit is not possible; that ceiling is the provider's.
- **`claude`**, not started. The harness has no lane for it yet.
- **The bead's own validity**, raised by glm twice and worth answering rather than dismissing.
- **A second subject.** Everything here is one bead, so it measures cost and variance on one problem, not capability.

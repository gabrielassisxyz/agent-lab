# The night of 2026-08-15: three lanes, and an instrument that had to be rebuilt around them

**What the night bought: the admission gate discriminates, and it is not at ceiling.** That was the pilot's central worry and the reason the whole experiment was in doubt. With three lanes and enough runs, the gate returns admitted, wrong, no-diff and truncated on the same bead, and one lane fails it in a consistent, identifiable way.

**What it cost: most of the hours went into the harness, not into the models.** Seven defects were found, every one of them in the environment rather than in a lane, and four of them would have quietly corrupted every number produced afterwards.

### gemini fails the same way every time

Every rejected agy run decodes in `set_on_link_find`, the callback that receives an **already-parsed** URL. `&amp;` survives that, because it is not a URL delimiter; `&#38;` and `&#x26;` do not, because a parser cuts at `#` before the callback ever sees the href. The verdict is always `a1 true, a2 false, a3 true, a4 false, a5 false` and the observed requests are always `/p/named?x=1&y=2` correct beside `/p/hex?x=1&` truncated.

This is precisely the architecture the pilot documented as the plausible-wrong answer, the one a hand-written probe fell into first. **The lane does not fail randomly; it fails into a specific wrong design, repeatedly.** That is a far more useful result than a pass rate.

### glm argues rather than implements

Two glm runs ended with `stopReason: stop` after 80 to 99 turns, having written no code, to argue that the bead's prescribed fix cannot meet its own acceptance criteria. Whether the objection is right is a separate question worth its own look - two other lanes did satisfy those criteria - but it is not a lane failing to reach an answer. It reached one and declined to implement it.

### kimi completes, and it is the expensive way to complete

Four for four on admission, at 122 to 474 turns against gemini's single turn. One of those runs is also the one that patched the machine's `spider` crate, so its turns include work spent inside a dependency, and its cost is not comparable with a run that stayed in the repository.

## What cannot be computed yet, and why not

**Cost per completed bead, across lanes.** The arithmetic is ready and the inputs are not comparable: `agy` reports one envelope total per run while `pi` sums per-turn input, which counts the whole prompt again every turn; and `agy` reports cache reads while the Ollama lanes do not report them at all. Dividing one by the other would produce a number, not a measurement. Within a lane the comparison holds, and the per-lane figures are in `runs.json`.

## The seven defects, and what each would have cost

| # | defect | what it would have done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Runs were worktrees sharing the subject's ref namespace | `git log --all` opened with earlier runs' fixes, naming both the fix and its layer |
| 2 | The rubric and the answer key were readable during a run | the jail was checked, the runs were launched outside it |
| 3 | The agy lane's credential was never copied | the lane failed auth in two seconds and produced no data point |
| 4 | The collector read token keys the harnesses do not write | a well-formed zero across 28 turns, which reads as a measurement |
| 5 | One scoring build directory across identical clones | one tree's binary ran another tree's test, stably and plausibly |
| 6 | A run patched `spider` in the shared cargo registry | the subject bead was fixed for every later build, including the scorer's |
| 7 | The build volume filled | runs failed instantly in a loop, each burning an id and a strike |

Defects 5 and 6 are the ones worth remembering. Both produce a **stable** wrong answer, and stability is what made them survive: four identical re-scores agreeing on a wrong verdict reads exactly like a solid measurement. What separated them from a real result was the negative control - score a tree known to be unfixed and confirm the check can fail - which is now the first step in the manual rather than a step in it.

## One rule the night produced

**Share what is immutable, isolate what gets written.** It arrived three times in three costumes: cargo's extracted sources, npm's bookkeeping, and the scorer's build directory. Every run isolates its own state and then deliberately shares the machine's caches, because an empty cache is a tax the first run pays and the rest do not. Sharing is right and it is not free, and the part that makes it expensive is that **the failure never implicates the cache** - it arrives as an accusation against the run: a model that cannot build, a lane that does not exist, a base tree that passes.

## Open

- **`deepseek`**, parked. The cause of its failure is fully measured: `pi` sends `max_completion_tokens`, which is silently ignored on the path to Ollama Cloud, so no output limit is applied and the model ran to the provider's hard 65 536 ceiling with `reasoning_effort: max` forced by the proxy. Raising the limit is not possible; that ceiling is the provider's.
- **`claude`**, not started. The harness has no lane for it yet.
- **The bead's own validity**, raised by glm twice and worth answering rather than dismissing.
- **A second subject.** Everything here is one bead, so it measures cost and variance on one problem, not capability.

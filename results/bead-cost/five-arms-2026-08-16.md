# Five arms on the cost bead, and the verdict that changed when the scorer was repaired

Twenty-six runs on `llmux` bead `llmux-p4-two-phase-reservation-5vg`, base `64cfb7e`. Five arms, five runs each, one lane per account where the account carries a rate ceiling.

**The headline is not the cost table.** It is that one arm silently deleted pre-existing public API in every single run, that the scorer as built could not see it, and that repairing the scorer moved that arm from four passes out of five to zero.

## What the arms cost

Every figure is read from the run artefacts. All twenty-six were re-scored and re-collected by one instrument after the repairs below, so nothing here is a mixture of two scorers.

| arm | passed | turns | output | reasoning | input | lines added | wall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| kimi-k2.7 / `pi` | **5/5** | 46 ± 12% | **13 034** ± 13% | 8 993 | 2.73 M | 152 ± 13% | 356 s |
| gemini-3.7-flash / `agy` | **5/5** | 62 ± 20% | 23 234 ± 15% | 14 974 | 297 k | 168 ± 10% | 232 s |
| sonnet / `claude` | **5/5** | 57 ± 21% | 32 637 ± 16% | 21 206 | 100 | 169 ± 15% | 448 s |
| deepseek pro-**HIGH** / `pi` | **5/5** | 45 ± 22% | 33 412 ± 23% | 24 049 | 3.18 M | 142 ± 9% | 427 s |
| deepseek pro-**MAX** / `pi` | **0/5** | 74 ± 20% | 74 959 ± 32% | 44 619 | 7.53 M | **443 ± 36%** | 836 s |

The `agy` turn count was absent when this page was first written and was recovered afterwards from that harness's trajectory database; its envelope reports 1 for a run of any length because it counts its own retries. Its five runs did 79, 66, 48, 66 and 52 model steps. Turns are now populated for every arm, which is what the metric with the most headroom in this experiment needed in order to be usable at all.

The one arm that reports money is `claude`, at **US$ 3.04 ± 0.58 of list price per completed bead**. That figure is carried under `list_price_usd_not_paid` because the lane authenticates with a subscription token: what was spent is subscription allowance, not dollars. The Ollama lanes publish no price at all, and none is invented for them here.

**Between the cheapest passing arm and the most expensive there is 2.6x in output tokens** (kimi 13 034, deepseek-high 33 412), against a run-to-run spread of 13 to 23 percent inside each arm. The separation is far outside the noise.

## `reasoning_effort: max` is worse than `high` on this task, on every axis measured

The two deepseek arms differ in exactly one setting. Same model (`openai/deepseek-v4-pro:cloud`), same `max_tokens`, same timeout, same rpm, one run per account in both. Only `reasoning_effort` changes.

| turns | 74 | 45 | 1.65x | 3.61 |
| output tokens | 74 959 | 33 412 | 2.24x | 3.70 |
| reasoning tokens | 44 619 | 24 049 | 1.86x | 2.97 |

With five runs an arm, those t values put every one of those differences outside the noise. **`max` costs more than twice as much and buys a worse result**, and the word "worse" is doing real work here rather than standing in for "not better".

## What `max` actually did, in all five runs

It deleted `ReserveRateSlot` and `FinalizeDispatch` from `Coordinator`. Both are pre-existing public methods, present at the base commit and **still present in the commit that implemented this bead for real**, so removing them is not something the task asked for or excused.

The separation across the campaign is total:

| arm | runs keeping both methods |
| --- | --- |
| kimi | 5 of 5 |
| agy | 5 of 5 |
| claude | 5 of 5 |
| deepseek-high | 5 of 5 |
| deepseek-**max** | **0 of 5** |

Twenty runs kept the package's contract; five broke it, and they were all the same arm. That is the shape of a lane-level behaviour, not of five unlucky rolls.

It also writes three times as much code to get there (443 lines against 142 to 169 everywhere else), with the widest spread of any arm.

## Why the old scorer could not see it, which is the part worth keeping

The canonical verification is vendored and overwrites the tree's copy, so a run cannot pass by weakening it. That much worked. What it missed is that the vendored file **calls helpers it does not define** - `reserveAndFinalize` lives in `rate_test.go`, `reserveFinalizeRelease` in `blackout_test.go` - and those files were left exactly as the run had edited them.

So a run could rename or delete production API, rewrite the sibling tests to match its new API, and still be graded solely against the canonical test. Which is what happened, five times out of five, and it scored 16/16 four of those times.

The repair is to restore **the whole test surface** of the graded package from the base before applying the vendored file. The verdict then depends on the production code and nothing else, which is what it always claimed to depend on.

**The ladder was re-run against the repaired scorer before any verdict was believed**, because a scorer change that is not laddered is a new wrong answer written over an old one:

```
base  64cfb7e   0/16   build_failed: true
gold  3d6a5a2  16/16
```

Gold passes because the commit that implemented this bead for real touches no test file in the package and removes no method. The bead never needed the API broken.

## Two more instrument repairs the campaign forced

**Scoring moved off the run's tree.** The scorer used to write the vendored fixture into the run's own checkout and leave it there. A run's tree is evidence: its implementation has to survive being graded, and graded again, since re-scoring from artefacts on disk is how a metric gets repaired without paying for the runs again. Grading now happens on a disposable copy. All twenty-seven trees were hashed before and after re-scoring every one of them: **no implementation changed**.

That also made `dirty` honest. It used to be true whenever the scorer had written into the tree, which is to say it reported the scorer rather than the agent.

**The collector could not see work git had not been told about.** It measured a change with `git diff`, so a run that solved the bead in a new file and never committed had all of it invisible. One run wrote 137 lines into a new file, was graded 16/16 on exactly those lines, and was recorded as a thirteen-line change. Two of twenty-six runs were affected; the `claude` arm's spread on lines added was 53 percent before the fix and 15 percent after. **The dispersion was the defect, not the model.**

## What constrains reading any of this

- **Wall clock is not comparable across arms.** They ran at different concurrency: `agy` one at a time, `kimi` four at once, the deepseek arms three then two. The column partly measures the queue.
- **`input` is not one quantity.** The `pi` lanes sum per turn, which counts the whole prompt again every turn; `agy` and `claude` report an envelope total. The claude arm's `100` and the deepseek arm's `7.5 M` are not on one scale. What only `agy` and `claude` publish is cache read (4.4 M and 6.2 M); the Ollama lanes do not report it at all, and a zero there is absence.
- ~~**`turns` is missing for `agy`.**~~ **Recovered.** Its envelope counts its own retries and reports 1 for every run; the collector now reads the model steps from the trajectory database, and the envelope's own figure is kept beside them under a name that says what it counts.
- **The claude arm mixes accounts.** Four runs on one subscription, the fifth on another after the first hit its session limit. `answered_by` is `claude-sonnet-5` on all five.
- **One claude run never reached the model** and is excluded rather than counted as a failure. A lane that could not be used and a model that got the task wrong mean opposite things.

## What this says about the bead

Twenty of twenty-five usable runs pass, and until the scorer was repaired it was twenty-four. `5vg` remains a **cost bead**: it is not there to separate lanes by capability, and it does not.

What it turns out to do, unasked, is separate them by **how much collateral damage they do on the way**. Four arms converge on 142 to 169 lines and keep the package's contract; one writes 443 and deletes two public methods. A binary verdict on the canonical test never showed that, and a graded verdict on the canonical test alone showed it backwards.

The qualitative half of the rubric - *was this the right layer?* - has still never been exercised. It now has an obvious first question to answer.

**Every implementation was afterwards audited with the toolchain rather than with a verdict**, and the other twenty proved clean on build, vet, the full suite, the race detector, the exported API and a baselined static-analysis scan: [`auditing-the-implementations-2026-08-16.md`](auditing-the-implementations-2026-08-16.md).
